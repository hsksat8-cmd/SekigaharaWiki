# Rear Unit: Deep Formation and Matchlock Ratio Comparison

[日本語版](../../../experiments/pincer/rear-deep-matchlock.md)

[Comprehensive Pincer Attack Experiments](index.md)

For the rear unit in a pincer attack, this experiment compares **30% and 45% Matchlock Ashigaru ratios in Deep formation**.

## Test Conditions

- Eastern Army: 12,200 troops
- Western frontal unit: 5,000 troops
  - Formation: Deep
  - Matchlock Ashigaru ratio: 30%
  - Tactic: Normal
- Western rear unit: 5,000 troops
  - Formation: Deep
  - Tactic: Charge
  - Matchlock Ashigaru ratio: 30% or 45%
- 10 battles for each condition
- Loss figures are shown as Eastern Army losses and total Western Army losses

## Results

| Rear unit | Western wins | Eastern wins | Avg. Eastern losses | Avg. Western losses | Loss exchange ratio* |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Deep 30% · Charge | 6 | 4 | 10,829 | 8,528 | 1.270 |
| Deep 45% · Charge | 6 | 4 | 11,178 | 8,145 | **1.372** |

*Eastern Army average losses divided by Western Army average losses. A higher value indicates a more favorable loss exchange for the Western Army.

Both conditions produced 6 Western Army wins in 10 battles, for a 60% win rate. Therefore, this experiment did not show a difference in win rate caused by the matchlock ratio.

The loss figures, however, showed a clear difference. Increasing the rear unit from 30% to 45% matchlocks **increased average Eastern Army losses by 349** and **reduced average Western Army losses by 383**.

## Results by Winner

| Rear unit | Eastern losses in Western wins | Western losses in Western wins | Eastern losses in Eastern wins | Western losses in Eastern wins |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Deep 30% · Charge | 12,200 | 8,298 | 8,773 | 8,873 |
| Deep 45% · Charge | 12,200 | 7,950 | 9,645 | 8,438 |

In battles won by the Western Army, both conditions inflicted an average of 12,200 Eastern Army losses, meaning the Eastern force was completely destroyed. However, Western Army average losses were **348 lower** with the 45% matchlock ratio.

Even in battles lost by the Western Army, the 45% condition inflicted an average of 9,645 losses on the Eastern Army. This was **872 more** than the 8,773 average under the 30% condition, while Western Army losses were also **435 lower**.

The 45% matchlock ratio therefore appears to have improved results mainly by **reducing losses in victories and inflicting heavier losses on the Eastern Army even in defeats**, rather than by increasing the win rate.

## Comparison with Rear Crane Wing

Compared with the earlier rear-unit test using the same frontal unit and **Crane Wing 45% · Charge** in the rear, the results were as follows.

| Rear unit | Western win rate | Avg. Eastern losses | Avg. Western losses | Loss exchange ratio |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Crane Wing 45% · Charge | 60% | 10,575 | 8,445 | 1.252 |
| Deep 30% · Charge | 60% | 10,829 | 8,528 | 1.270 |
| Deep 45% · Charge | 60% | **11,178** | **8,145** | **1.372** |

All three conditions produced a 60% Western Army win rate, but **Deep 45%** inflicted the highest average losses on the Eastern Army while keeping Western Army losses the lowest.

Compared with Crane Wing 45%, Deep 45% **increased average Eastern Army losses by 603** and **reduced average Western Army losses by 300**.

## Analysis

In Deep formation, the spear ashigaru and cavalry at the front engage in close combat while the larger concentration of Matchlock Ashigaru in the rear continues firing. Because the rear unit is set to **Charge**, the unit as a whole maintains offensive pressure while forming vertical layers of frontline troops and firing troops.

Crane Wing spreads troops across both wings and can encircle the enemy over a broad area. Under the conditions tested here, however, where the rear unit continuously applies pressure to the enemy from behind, concentrating troops in depth may have produced a more favorable loss exchange.

## Summary

Increasing the rear unit's Matchlock Ashigaru ratio from 30% to 45% did not change the Western Army win rate, which remained at 60%.

However, **Deep 45% · Charge** produced a better loss exchange than both Deep 30% · Charge and Crane Wing 45% · Charge. Western losses were lower in victories, and the unit still inflicted heavy losses on the Eastern Army in defeats.

Under these conditions, the benefit of using **Deep 45% · Charge** for the rear unit appeared more clearly in **combat efficiency and loss stability than in an increased win rate**.

Each condition was tested in 10 battles. More trials are needed before drawing conclusions about differences in win rate.
