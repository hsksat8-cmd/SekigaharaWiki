# Pincer Attack Experiment

[日本語版](../../formations/pincer-experiment.md)

![Pincer attack experiment deployment](../../assets/images/pincer-experiment-layout.png){ .wiki-image }
<p class="image-caption">Pincer attack experiment deployment. User Commander 2 on the left and User Commander 1 in the center engage each other, while User Commander 3 on the right attacks the central unit from behind.</p>

A total of **118 trials** were conducted to compare how different formations perform in a pincer attack.

In the compiled results, the Western Army's 10,000 troops are divided into a **5,000-troop frontal force and a 5,000-troop rear force**, attacking an Eastern Army force of either 10,000 or 15,000 troops in the center.

The **exchange efficiency** used below is **Eastern Army losses ÷ Western Army losses**. A higher value indicates a more favorable loss exchange for the Western Army.

## Overall Results

| Eastern Army troops | Trials | Eastern wins | Western wins |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 10,000 | 38 | 0 | 38 |
| 15,000 | 80 | 80 | 0 |

The strongest overall factor was the difference in troop strength.

- At 10,000 vs. 10,000, the Western Army pincer force won every trial.
- When the Eastern Army had 15,000 troops, the Eastern Army won every trial.
- Formation and matchlock proportion substantially changed the amount of losses, but under these test conditions none reversed the winner.

## Eastern Army 10,000: Frontal Force Fixed at Flexible Square 30%

The frontal force was fixed at **Flexible Square, 30% Matchlock Ashigaru, 5,000 troops**, while the formation and matchlock proportion of the 5,000-troop rear force were changed. The Eastern Army lost all 10,000 troops in every one of the 38 trials.

| Rear force | Trials | Avg. Western losses | Exchange efficiency |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Crane Wing 45% | 9 | 6,803 | **1.470** |
| Deep 45% | 5 | 6,886 | 1.452 |
| Flexible Square 45% | 4 | 7,083 | 1.412 |
| Fish Scale 45% | 4 | 7,085 | 1.411 |
| Crane Wing 30% | 5 | 7,172 | 1.394 |
| Flexible Square 30% | 6 | 7,622 | 1.312 |
| Deep 30% | 5 | 7,938 | 1.260 |

For the same formation, a 45% matchlock proportion in the rear force reduced Western Army losses compared with 30%.

- Crane Wing: 369 fewer losses at 45% than at 30%
- Flexible Square: 539 fewer
- Deep: 1,052 fewer

In equal-strength battles, the improved survivability of a 45% matchlock rear force appeared consistently.

## Eastern Army 15,000: Frontal Force Fixed at Flexible Square 30%

| Rear force | Trials | Avg. Eastern losses | Avg. Western losses | Exchange efficiency | Loss difference |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Crane Wing 45% | 5 | 7,154 | 7,668 | **0.933** | 514 |
| Deep 45% | 5 | 7,156 | 7,816 | 0.916 | 660 |
| Fish Scale 45% | 5 | 6,988 | 7,650 | 0.913 | 662 |
| Crane Wing 60% | 5 | 6,154 | 6,758 | 0.911 | 604 |
| Deep 30% | 5 | 7,604 | 8,358 | 0.910 | 754 |
| Flexible Square 30% | 10 | 7,639 | 8,609 | 0.887 | 970 |
| Flexible Square 45% | 5 | 5,990 | 7,664 | 0.782 | 1,674 |

The following tendencies were observed.

- Rear Crane Wing 45% had the highest exchange efficiency.
- Rear Deep 45% and Fish Scale 45% had similar average performance.
- Rear Fish Scale 45% produced especially stable Western Army losses.
- Rear Crane Wing 60% reduced losses on both sides.
- Rear Flexible Square 30% produced heavy losses for both sides.
- Rear Flexible Square 45% inflicted relatively low Eastern losses compared with its Western losses.

Increasing Crane Wing from 45% to 60% matchlocks reduced average Western losses by 910, but also reduced average Eastern losses inflicted by 1,000.

## Eastern Army 15,000: Rear Force Fixed at Crane Wing 45%

The rear force was fixed at **Crane Wing, 45% Matchlock Ashigaru, 5,000 troops**, while the formation and matchlock proportion of the frontal force were changed.

| Frontal force | Trials | Avg. Eastern losses | Avg. Western losses | Exchange efficiency | Loss difference |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Deep 30% | 5 | 8,296 | 8,508 | **0.975** | 212 |
| Flexible Square 15% | 5 | 7,220 | 7,526 | 0.959 | 306 |
| Fish Scale 30% | 5 | 7,576 | 7,934 | 0.955 | 358 |
| Deep 15% | 5 | 7,198 | 7,706 | 0.934 | 508 |
| Flexible Square 30% | 5 | 7,154 | 7,668 | 0.933 | 514 |
| Deep 45% | 5 | 6,342 | 7,438 | 0.853 | 1,096 |
| Fish Scale 15% | 5 | 6,056 | 7,346 | 0.824 | 1,290 |
| Crane Wing 45% | 5 | 6,618 | 8,126 | 0.814 | 1,508 |
| Flexible Square 0% | 5 | 4,352 | 7,422 | 0.586 | 3,070 |

### Flexible Square

| Matchlock proportion | Avg. Eastern losses | Avg. Western losses | Exchange efficiency |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0% | 4,352 | 7,422 | 0.586 |
| 15% | 7,220 | 7,526 | **0.959** |
| 30% | 7,154 | 7,668 | 0.933 |

Performance improved sharply from 0% to 15%. At 0%, Western losses were only 104 lower than at 15%, while Eastern losses inflicted were 2,868 lower.

The 15% and 30% results were close, but 15% was slightly better in both average Western losses and exchange efficiency.

### Deep

| Matchlock proportion | Avg. Eastern losses | Avg. Western losses | Exchange efficiency |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 15% | 7,198 | 7,706 | 0.934 |
| 30% | 8,296 | 8,508 | **0.975** |
| 45% | 6,342 | 7,438 | 0.853 |

From 15% to 30%, losses increased on both sides while exchange efficiency also improved. From 30% to 45%, losses on both sides fell, with Eastern losses falling especially sharply.

The relationship was not monotonic and showed a peak at 30%.

### Fish Scale

| Matchlock proportion | Avg. Eastern losses | Avg. Western losses | Exchange efficiency |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 15% | 6,056 | 7,346 | 0.824 |
| 30% | 7,576 | 7,934 | **0.955** |

At 30%, average Eastern losses were 1,520 higher than at 15%, while Western losses were 588 higher. Exchange efficiency improved substantially.

However, Fish Scale 30% showed high variability, with Eastern losses ranging from 5,450 to 8,740. Excluding one failed battle, the other four trials produced an average loss exchange close to 1:1.

### Crane Wing 45% on Both Front and Rear

The average result was **6,618 Eastern losses, 8,126 Western losses, and an exchange efficiency of 0.814**.

Compared with Flexible Square 30% in front plus Crane Wing 45% in the rear:

- Eastern losses were 536 lower.
- Western losses were 458 higher.
- The loss difference widened from 514 to 1,508.

Rather than using Crane Wing 45% for both the front and rear, the **asymmetric combination of Flexible Square 30% in front and Crane Wing 45% in the rear** produced the better loss exchange.

## Observations by Formation

### Flexible Square

In the frontal position, performance dropped sharply at 0% matchlocks. The 15% and 30% results were close, with 15% producing lower Western losses.

### Deep

Front Deep 15% had low variability. At 30%, total losses and exchange efficiency increased. At 45%, performance declined again.

### Fish Scale

Front Fish Scale 15% did not inflict enough damage on the Eastern Army, while 30% achieved one of the higher exchange efficiencies. At the same time, result variability also increased.

Rear Fish Scale 45% had average performance close to Deep 45%, with stable Western losses.

### Crane Wing

Rear Crane Wing 45% achieved high exchange efficiency in combination with several frontal formations. At 60%, losses fell on both sides.

When Crane Wing 45% was used for both the front and rear, exchange efficiency was lower than when the frontal force used Flexible Square or Deep.

## Variability

Especially stable conditions were:

- Front Deep 15%: standard deviation of Eastern losses about 193 and Western losses about 144
- Rear Fish Scale 45%: standard deviation of Western losses about 91
- Front Flexible Square 0%: low performance, but stable results

Conditions with high variability were:

- Front Fish Scale 30%: standard deviation of Eastern losses about 1,166
- Front Deep 30%: standard deviation of Eastern losses about 767
- Front Flexible Square 30% + rear Flexible Square 30%: large differences between trial groups

## Summary

The pincer attack experiment confirmed the following points under the tested conditions.

- The winner was completely separated by whether the Eastern Army had 10,000 or 15,000 troops.
- Performance changed substantially with matchlock proportion even within the same formation.
- The relationship between matchlock proportion and performance was not monotonic.
- The frontal force needed both close-combat troops to hold the enemy and ranged firepower.
- At 0% matchlocks, damage inflicted on the Eastern Army fell sharply even though more close-combat troops were available.
- At 45–60% matchlocks, many conditions reduced losses on both sides.
- The same formation performed differently in the frontal and rear roles.
- Even when the Western Army achieved the better loss exchange in some trials, the Eastern Army still won every 15,000-troop battle.
- The highest average exchange efficiency against an Eastern Army force of 15,000 was 0.975, which was not enough to overturn the numerical disadvantage through attrition alone.
