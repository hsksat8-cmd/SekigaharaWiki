# Formation and Matchlock Ashigaru Experiments

[日本語版](../../formations/experiments.md)

Using the **Formation Experiment 1** scenario, these tests compare how different combinations of **matchlock ashigaru proportion and formation** affect battle results.

The tests do not exhaustively compare every matchlock proportion from 0% to 70%. They focus mainly on two sets of conditions:

- Equal-strength battles comparing **45% matchlock ashigaru vs. 30%**, with all five formations matched against each other
- Unequal-strength battles with **30% matchlock ashigaru on both sides**, changing only the formation of User Commander 1

This page therefore shows the suitability of different matchlock proportions for each formation and the formation matchups under the current game settings. It does not prove an optimal value for every possible matchlock proportion.

## Common Conditions

| Item | Condition |
|---|---|
| Scenario | Formation Experiment 1 |
| Soldiers per dot | 10 |
| Trials | 10 for each condition |
| Maximum decision turn | 720 |
| Result format | Commander 1 wins–Commander 2 wins / average decision turn / average remaining troops (Commander 1–Commander 2) |

Conditions with an average decision turn of 720.0 include trials that continued until the **8 PM troop-strength advantage decision** rather than ending through unit destruction.

## Equal-Strength Battle Analysis

### Crane Wing works well with a higher matchlock proportion

With 45% matchlock ashigaru, Crane Wing posted strong winning records against Square, Flexible Square, and Deep. It went **10–0** against both Flexible Square and Deep.

Crane Wing makes it easier to maintain a wide frontage, so matchlock ashigaru are less likely to have their line of fire blocked by friendly troops in front of them. When the enemy concentrates in the center, large numbers of matchlock troops on both wings can also take part in the shooting.

### Deep has difficulty using a high matchlock proportion

Deep went **1–49** across 50 trials.

Its narrow frontage makes it easier for rear matchlock troops to lose their line of fire once the front ranks enter close combat. A high matchlock proportion also leaves fewer spear ashigaru and cavalry available to hold the front line and provide protection.

Deep is better suited to breakthrough and close-combat-oriented tactics than to a high matchlock proportion.

### Fish Scale can serve as an offensive counter

Fish Scale posted winning records against Square, Flexible Square, and Deep, but went **4–6** against Crane Wing.

Fish Scale tends to advance and force close combat, which can reduce the enemy matchlock troops' opportunities to fire. However, if it cannot break through Crane Wing's broad front, it may continue taking fire while advancing.

### Square and Flexible Square are stable, but weaker against firepower-focused formations

Square and Flexible Square performed well against Deep but were disadvantaged against Crane Wing and Fish Scale.

They are useful for defensive or balanced play, but in these tests they did not gain as much from a high matchlock proportion as Crane Wing did.

Even in matchups using the **same formation**, User Commander 1 was not always favored. Results are affected not only by the difference in matchlock proportion, but also by starting position, direction of advance, the order in which firing lines form, and the placement of cavalry and protecting troops.

## Unequal-Strength Battle Analysis

When one side had twice the troop strength and both armies used 30% matchlock ashigaru, **formation alone did not overturn the numerical advantage**.

- The 5,000-troop side lost all 50 trials
- The 10,000-troop side won all 50 trials
- On the weaker side, Fish Scale reduced the opponent's average remaining troop count the most
- On the stronger side, Crane Wing achieved the fastest decisions and the highest average remaining troop count
- Deep produced relatively fast decisions, but even on the stronger side it left fewer troops remaining, indicating heavier close-combat losses

Under these conditions, the choice of formation affected **how quickly the battle was decided and how severe the losses were more than it affected the winner**.

## Suggested Matchlock Ashigaru Proportions

Based on these results, the following ranges are useful guidelines for each formation.

| Formation | Suggested matchlock proportion | Main use |
|---|---:|---|
| Square | 25–35% | Defensive and stable operations |
| Flexible Square | 25–35% | Standard and general-purpose operations |
| Crane Wing | **40–50%** | Firefights and firepower across a broad front |
| Deep | **0–20%** | Breakthroughs and close combat |
| Fish Scale | 20–30% | Forward pressure and offensive operations |

The current default proposal of **45% for Crane Wing, 15% for Deep, and 30% for the other formations** is consistent with these experiment results.

Matchlock ashigaru do not become stronger simply by increasing their numbers. A high matchlock proportion becomes effective when it is combined with a broad field of fire, protection from spear ashigaru and cavalry, and the ability to hold the front line when the enemy closes in.
