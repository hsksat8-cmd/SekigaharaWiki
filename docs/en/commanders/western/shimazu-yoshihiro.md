This is a medium-sized unit, with notably high leadership (94) and attack (92). Starting morale is 109, while hesitation is 56.

## Brief Career

A veteran of decades of warfare in Kyushu, Yoshihiro stood on the Western side at Sekigahara. His force's dangerous withdrawal through enemy lines, later celebrated as the Shimazu retreat, became a defining episode of the battle.

## Character and Reputation

Courage and recklessness are not the same. His apparent concern for routes of escape and the survival of his men offers an important perspective on the judgment of a commander hardened by repeated crises.

## The Person and Their Background

Long experience of warfare in Kyushu had confronted Yoshihiro not only with frontal combat but also with the problem of keeping men alive when outmatched. His reputation for bravery may have rested as much on his ability to keep making decisions in danger as on his willingness to face it. Experience mattered especially when troop numbers and coordination with allies failed to meet expectations.

The withdrawal from Sekigahara is remembered as a desperate attempt to break through enemy forces. When victory was no longer possible, the question became what could still be saved. Choosing survival and the preservation of one's house, rather than only an honorable death, could also demand a warrior's resolve.
